# Reading and writing Open Bible Stories

Budget (task for `tc-obs`, docs/PERFORMANCE.md rules 1 and 2): parsing all 50 stories and
their frames costs what it reads. PERFORMANCE.md has no OBS row; the nearest rows are the
Editor's "open a book: the first chapter's verses ready in 50 ms" and "save one verse: the
chapter's projection 2 ms".

## What is counted

`crates/tc-obs/tests/performance.rs` counts the story lines each action touches
(`tc_obs::story::lines_touched`): each line a read splits off, and each line a writer, the
decoder or a projection puts out. It runs on the fifty stories of the en_obs v9 export
(`bundle/en/en_obs_v9.sb.zip`, 190,293 bytes, 2,592 lines) and on the same stories made ten
times larger (each story's frames ten times over, in the writers' layout: 1,903,543 bytes,
24,120 lines).
On both corpora:

| Action | Lines touched |
|---|---|
| Read every story and its frames (`StoryFile::parse` and `story()`) | exactly the lines read |
| Open one story | that story's lines, whatever the other stories hold |
| Decode a story into its records | the story's lines, and its base's lines written once |
| Render the records back | the base's lines, and the story's lines written once |
| Save one frame (`write_frame`) | that story's lines, read once and written once |

The decoder writes its base in one pass, each line once. A first version reduced each
recorded frame with a splice that moved every line after it, and the count, which then held
only the lines read, did not show it: a review measured 1.9 s to decode one story of 32,000
frames with two-line paragraphs. `decoding_one_long_story_costs_its_lines` checks one story
of 400 and of 4,000 such frames, and the timings below include stories of 2,000 and 32,000.

tC4's `applyStoryState` (`journal/story.mjs:184-190`) runs one writer per part, and each
writer reads the whole story again, so projecting a story there costs its frames times its
lines. `tc_obs::story::apply_story_state` reads the base once and splices every part in
one pass; a property test checks that it gives the bytes and refusals of the writers
applied one by one.

## Timings

`crates/tc-obs/tests/performance.rs`, test `the_obs_actions_are_quick`: one untimed
warm-up, then 41 runs of each action, median and p95. The stories are in memory before the
clock starts.

Commit `98ef676` (branch `domain-obs`), release build, rustc 1.97.0, Linux 6.18, 4 vCPU
Intel Xeon @ 2.80 GHz with AVX-512 and without SHA instructions, 15 GiB RAM, shared with
other agents' builds (load average 2.4 to 3.2 on the 4 CPUs during the runs). Five
separate processes, 2026-10-16:

| Action | Process 1 | Process 2 | Process 3 | Process 4 | Process 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read all 50 stories (Home tiles, progress) | 315 / 506 µs | 282 / 361 µs | 302 / 413 µs | 282 / 372 µs | 284 / 335 µs |
| Read all 50 stories, ten times larger | 3.11 / 5.20 ms | 2.99 / 3.37 ms | 3.28 / 7.52 ms | 3.09 / 4.34 ms | 3.07 / 4.97 ms |
| Open the largest story (6,805 bytes, 18 frames) | 7.5 / 7.6 µs | 7.5 / 8.1 µs | 7.5 / 7.9 µs | 7.4 / 7.7 µs | 7.7 / 11.5 µs |
| Save a frame of the largest story | 4.6 / 9.2 µs | 4.6 / 4.7 µs | 4.7 / 9.1 µs | 4.6 / 5.3 µs | 4.8 / 7.3 µs |
| Project the largest story from its records | 8.3 / 11.3 µs | 8.2 / 8.7 µs | 8.2 / 9.8 µs | 8.3 / 11.2 µs | 8.2 / 13.9 µs |
| Project all 50 stories from their records | 384 / 445 µs | 368 / 405 µs | 380 / 408 µs | 391 / 458 µs | 384 / 450 µs |
| Decode all 50 stories into records | 337 / 369 µs | 333 / 391 µs | 346 / 429 µs | 351 / 442 µs | 338 / 367 µs |
| Decode one story of 2,000 two-line frames | 632 / 681 µs | 627 / 673 µs | 628 / 741 µs | 657 / 733 µs | 634 / 689 µs |
| Decode one story of 32,000 two-line frames | 11.2 / 16.0 ms | 11.4 / 20.7 ms | 11.1 / 15.3 ms | 11.6 / 14.3 ms | 11.4 / 14.1 ms |

Each cell is median / p95.

Reading every story takes about 0.3 ms, a hundred and fifty times under the 50 ms of the
nearest budget; one story's projection takes about 8 µs against 2 ms. The ten-times corpus
holds 10.0 times the bytes and 9.3 times the lines, and takes 10.6 times as long (2.99 ms
against 0.28 ms, process 2): the time follows the bytes read, the linear cost the counts
show. Decoding one story of 32,000 frames takes 18 times as long as one of 2,000 (11.4 ms
against 0.63 ms), for 16 times the lines. The decoder of commit `1627ece`, which spliced
once per frame, was timed on the same stories in the same build (the best of five runs,
next to the current decoder's): 4.4 ms against 0.61 ms for 2,000 frames, 58 ms against
2.7 ms for 8,000, and 1,837 ms against 13.0 ms for 32,000. Decoding the 50 stories went
from about 400 µs to about 340 µs. The spread of the p95 follows the load
from other builds on the machine. The test fails in a release build only above three times
the nearest budgets (PERFORMANCE.md rule 5): 150 ms to read all 50 stories, 6 ms to open
the largest story, save one of its frames, or project it.

To reproduce:

```sh
cargo test --release -p tc-obs --test performance -- --nocapture --test-threads 1
```
