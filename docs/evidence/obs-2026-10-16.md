# Reading and writing Open Bible Stories

Budget (task for `tc-obs`, docs/PERFORMANCE.md rules 1 and 2): parsing all 50 stories and
their frames costs what it reads. PERFORMANCE.md has no OBS row; the nearest rows are the
Editor's "open a book: the first chapter's verses ready in 50 ms" and "save one verse: the
chapter's projection 2 ms".

## What is counted

`crates/tc-obs/tests/performance.rs` counts the story lines each action examines
(`tc_obs::story::lines_examined`), on the fifty stories of the en_obs v9 export
(`bundle/en/en_obs_v9.sb.zip`, 190,293 bytes, 2,592 lines) and on the same stories made ten
times larger (each story's frames ten times over, in the writers' layout: 1,903,543 bytes,
24,120 lines).
On both corpora:

| Action | Lines examined |
|---|---|
| Read every story and its frames (`StoryFile::parse` and `story()`) | exactly the lines read |
| Open one story | that story's lines, whatever the other stories hold |
| Decode a story into its records and render them back | the story's lines and its base's lines, once each |
| Save one frame (`write_frame`) | that story's lines |

tC4's `applyStoryState` (`journal/story.mjs:184-190`) runs one writer per part, and each
writer reads the whole story again, so projecting a story there costs its frames times its
lines. `tc_obs::story::apply_story_state` reads the base once and splices every part in
one pass; a property test checks that it gives the bytes and refusals of the writers
applied one by one.

## Timings

`crates/tc-obs/tests/performance.rs`, test `the_obs_actions_are_quick`: one untimed
warm-up, then 41 runs of each action, median and p95. The stories are in memory before the
clock starts.

Commit `2992bc2` (branch `domain-obs`), release build, rustc 1.97.0, Linux 6.18, 4 vCPU
Intel Xeon @ 2.80 GHz with AVX-512 and without SHA instructions, 15 GiB RAM, shared with
other agents' builds (load average 7.0 on the 4 CPUs during the runs). Five separate
processes, 2026-10-16:

| Action | Process 1 | Process 2 | Process 3 | Process 4 | Process 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read all 50 stories (Home tiles, progress) | 315 / 489 µs | 385 / 522 µs | 287 / 368 µs | 291 / 461 µs | 351 / 480 µs |
| Read all 50 stories, ten times larger | 2.95 / 3.31 ms | 3.04 / 4.00 ms | 2.96 / 3.40 ms | 2.96 / 3.58 ms | 3.17 / 4.90 ms |
| Open the largest story (6,805 bytes, 18 frames) | 7.4 / 8.2 µs | 7.4 / 7.6 µs | 7.4 / 8.0 µs | 7.4 / 8.0 µs | 7.4 / 8.6 µs |
| Save a frame of the largest story | 7.5 / 8.8 µs | 4.6 / 4.8 µs | 4.6 / 4.7 µs | 4.6 / 4.7 µs | 4.7 / 4.8 µs |
| Project the largest story from its records | 15.9 / 18.6 µs | 8.1 / 10.1 µs | 8.2 / 9.0 µs | 8.4 / 11.1 µs | 8.3 / 13.5 µs |
| Project all 50 stories from their records | 375 / 448 µs | 393 / 636 µs | 373 / 421 µs | 366 / 441 µs | 378 / 493 µs |
| Decode all 50 stories into records | 395 / 444 µs | 420 / 635 µs | 411 / 500 µs | 395 / 451 µs | 399 / 477 µs |

Each cell is median / p95.

Reading every story takes about 0.3 ms, a hundred and fifty times under the 50 ms of the
nearest budget; one story's projection takes about 8 µs against 2 ms. The ten-times corpus
holds 10.0 times the bytes and 9.3 times the lines, and takes 10.3 times as long (2.96 ms
against 0.29 ms, process 3): the time follows the bytes read, the linear cost the counts
show. The spread of the p95 follows the load from other builds on
the machine. The test fails in a release build only above three times the nearest budgets
(PERFORMANCE.md rule 5): 150 ms to read all 50 stories, 6 ms to open the largest story,
save one of its frames, or project it.

To reproduce:

```sh
cargo test --release -p tc-obs --test performance -- --nocapture --test-threads 1
```
