# Ruling H5: only verse text conflicts; everything else, the newest wins

- Asked: 2026-10-24, by the roadmap session, with the C7b packet of 20 conflict-review screens
  (`docs/evidence/q9-2026-10-24.md` on `track-C7b`).
- Answer, in the owner's words: "Checks and alignment are simple. the newest one wins. Only text
  changes conflict."
- Asked again about the two cases where the newest version could lose work, the owner said: "If
  it's saved in history, how will the user get them back. I assume you can tell a deleted book
  from one that the other user hasn't added yet. We may have to changed the audio decision later,
  but for now, newest wins, keep both."

## The rule

1. **Only verse text (and story frame text) conflicts.** C7a's Text tab stays as built.
2. **Everything else: the newest version wins, with no question asked.** This covers check
   decisions, moved checks, alignments, comments, project settings, resource pins, a book removed
   or brought back, and path-case clashes.
   - "Newest" means the change made last, by the time the change records. A tie goes to the
     change with the higher change id, so every computer picks the same one.
   - The older version is not lost. It stays in History, and History's *Restore this version*
     brings it back.
3. **Carried files (audio and other files): the newest wins, and both are kept.** The newest file
   keeps its name. The other is saved beside it under the other person's name, for example
   `jon_01-Juan.wav`, with a name the project doesn't hold yet. Nothing is asked. The owner may
   change this later.
4. **Books.** A book a teammate removed is told apart from a book another computer never added: a
   removal is a recorded change, and a book never added has no record. If a removal is the newest,
   the book is removed. The edits made to it stay in History. Adding the book back offers to bring
   back its text, asked once (H22). If an edit is the newest, the book stays, with the edit.
5. **The last book.** A project never ends up with no books. A removal that would leave none loses
   to the edit, and the book stays.

## How a translator gets an older version back

- **A verse, a check, an alignment, a comment or a setting:** open History on it, and choose
  *Restore this version* on the version they want.
- **A removed book:** add the book again. The app asks once whether to bring back its text.
- **An audio file:** both files are in the project folder, the other one under the teammate's name.

## Effect on cards

- **C7b shrinks.** It no longer builds the Alignments, Checks or Settings conflict tabs, or the
  per-kind choice cards.
  - It keeps History with *Restore this version*, the Detached and Notices lists, the Received
    sheet (E2 needs it), the read-only banner, the draft offer, the boot error and the
    first-launch failure.
  - The Received sheet says what the newest version replaced, for example "Juan's alignment of
    Titus 1:1 replaced yours. Your version is in History." That way no silent change goes
    unnoticed.
- **The store and fold:** the same newest-wins rule replaces the conflict records for these kinds.
  Property tests prove that every computer converges on the same winner, and that the loser stays
  in History.
- **H19 and H22 part 2:** where they asked the translator to choose for carried files, rule 3
  replaces that.
