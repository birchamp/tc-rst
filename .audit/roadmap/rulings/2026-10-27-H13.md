# H13: A project imported with no versification file: guess the scheme from its contents and say so, or use English as tC4 does?

- Asked: 2026-10-22 in `ROADMAP.md`'s owner questions; put again on 2026-10-27 by the tC4 issue
  sweep, which found tC4 #822 reachable in 4.1.0 through the tC3 import
  (`docs/evidence/tc4-issues-2026-10-27/open.md` on branch `tc4-issues-2026-10-27`).
- Options: guess the scheme from the contents and say which (recommended); or use English as
  tC4 does.
- Answer: relayed by the orchestrator session on 2026-10-27: "The owner wants all 5 in the
  preview, so #822 is in too. … Follow the issue's proposal: the tC3 import guesses the scheme
  from verse counts across the six schemes and shows the guess as the default, and the translator
  can change it. A tie gives eng with a note saying the content didn't decide. Update a book keeps
  the project's scheme with no choice."

## The rule

- The tC3 import compares the verse counts of the books it holds with each of the six schemes
  (`eng`, `lxx`, `org`, `rsc`, `rso`, `vul`) and offers the one that fits best as the default on
  its review page. The translator can choose another before Import.
- When two or more schemes fit equally, the default is `eng`, and the page says the content did
  not decide the scheme.
- Update a book uses the project's scheme and shows no choice.
- The project gets the scheme shown when Import is pressed, in `ingredients/vrs.json` and in its
  first `project.vrs.set`.

## Effect for translators

A tC3 project whose books are numbered another way (for example Psalm titles as verse 1, or
Malachi with 3 chapters) keeps its source verses lined up with its draft. The scheme is fixed for
the life of the project, so the choice is made once, at the import, where it can be seen.
